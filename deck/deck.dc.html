03 / 11
WHY TOWERS CONFLICT

Nothing is wrong with the towers.
The conflict lives at the receiver

/ 01
A channel is a language

Everyone shouting in the same language interferes with everyone else in it. Any tower can tune to any carrier — there is no per-tower incompatibility.

/ 02
Two overlapping towers conflict

Mid-overlap, both towers arrive equally loud: SINR ≈ 0 dB. Raising power lifts signal and interference together; the phone hears the other tower as noise.

/ 03
Still true in 5G

Modern networks run reuse-1 and manage interference in the scheduler: two conflicting cells cannot share a resource block in the same subframe. Same graph, one layer down.

Two towers conflict exactly when some point exists where the interferer is above the noise floor while the server is still in range. Overlapping coverage discs are exactly that condition.

WHAT WE ARE SOLVING

A district of towers. A handful of licensed channels.

Which towers can transmit on the same channel — with every phone still able to decode?

Picture a hall where everyone has to shout. Stand near one shouter and you can follow them. Stand between two, and both arrive equally loud, so you follow neither. Towers are the shouters, phones are the listeners, and a channel is the language they shout in.

Draw every tower as a dot, and a line between any two that would ruin each other. Now the question is just: pick as many dots as you can with no line between them.

TOO AGGRESSIVE
Get it wrong
Phones in the overlap get nothing.
TOO CAUTIOUS
Get it safe
A channel each, and the spectrum bill explodes.
WHAT WE WANT
Get it right
The most towers on one channel, cleanly.
05 / 11
FORMULATION

This problem already has a name:
Maximum Independent Set.

When two towers overlap, we draw an edge between them. Now it is a graph, and the question is a maths one: find the largest set of dots with no edge between any two. That set is channel 1.

It is NP-hard, and solving it exactly means holding the whole cluster in view at once.

Why this is the hard part

The choices interlock. Pick one tower and its neighbours are out, which frees their neighbours, and so on across the district. A local rule never sees that chain.

06 / 11
FROM ONE CHANNEL TO A FULL PLAN

One MIS is a single channel.
Repeat until every tower has one.

The leftovers are not towers that can never share. They just missed channel 1. Most of them get along fine with each other.

So take the set, remove it, and run again on what is left. Each round is one channel. The number of rounds is how much spectrum the district needs.

To Note

Going greedy does not guarantee the fewest channels. On a worked 26-tower example it needs 5 where 4 would do, because grabbing the biggest set first strands the busiest tower. Finding the true minimum is a harder problem again.

THE EXPERIMENT

Watthana district, on 90 atoms.

Real OpenCelliD data for Watthana: 3,401 True-group LTE and UMTS cells, collapsed to 851 masts, then merged at the 238 m floor to 116 atoms. The densest 90 fit inside Aquila's 75 µm field, and those are the ones we run.

Positions are crowdsourced estimates, not surveyed towers, and the interference radius is a fixed 500 m rather than OpenCelliD's range column.

The hardware

QuEra Aquila, 256 neutral rubidium atoms, submitted through qBraid Lab. A quasi-adiabatic detuning sweep over 4 µs, 1,000 shots per task.

11 / 11
ASSUMPTIONS

Assumptions for the presentation

•Protocol model, not physical model. Conflicts are binary and pairwise. Real interference aggregates — twenty towers each below the noise floor can sum above it — and fails on a threshold ratio, not equality.
•Discs are not discs. Sectorised antennas radiate lobes and high-rise Bangkok is anisotropic. Unit disks are the abstraction — the same one Rydberg blockade implements natively.
•Fixed interference radius. OpenCelliD's range column is measurement confidence, not coverage. We use a fixed 500 m urban-LTE radius; masts within ~240 m are merged to fit the 4 µm atom floor.
•Crowdsourced positions. OpenCelliD estimated centroids, not surveyed towers. Colocated sectors are collapsed to one mast per site.
•Greedy colouring is not optimal. Repeated-MIS spent 5 channels on the worked 26-tower instance where 4 suffice. Minimum colouring is its own, harder problem — an exact ILP runs alongside.
•No quantum advantage claimed. CBC solves a 100-node MIS in milliseconds. The demo's value: the constraint is not encoded but physical — the blockade circles are the interference circles.
